The science4 drawers
Why frogs?

A male Japanese tree frog (Hyla japonica) calls almost periodically, and he hears his neighbours. Two males settle into calling almost exactly half a cycle apart (Aihara, Phys. Rev. E 2009). Three males cannot all alternate with one another, so the trio is frustrated and falls into richer patterns such as three-phase synchronisation (Aihara et al., Phys. Rev. E 2011). Field recordings of whole choruses most often show two-cluster anti-synchronisation (Aihara et al., Sci. Rep. 2014).

That makes a chorus a textbook system of coupled oscillators. Here every frog follows the Kuramoto phase model: a positive coupling pulls a pair into step, and a negative one pushes it half a cycle apart. The bank of this pond is a ring with an even number of frogs, so they can alternate all the way round. Five inlets close triangles, where alternation is frustrated, like the trios in the 2011 paper.

In the scene, a frog's throat (its vocal sac) puffs exactly when the model makes it call, and a dot runs along each thread to the frogs that hear it, brighter when they listen harder (coupling × lock). Ripples, blinks, hops, the moon and the reeds are decoration.

What the quantum engine did

Atlas qdrive-api-v1 builds a circuit from target expectation values instead of gates. Each lily pad is one qubit. We asked each qubit to start at its own phase on the equator of its Bloch sphere (a golden-angle spread, so no neighbours start in step). Then we asked every hearing edge for a lock: <XX> = <YY> = −0.7 (alternating pond) or +0.7 (sync pond). For two separately phased qubits, <XX> + <YY> equals the cosine of their phase difference. That is the same quantity the Kuramoto model pulls on.

qdrive applies targets in order and only re-measures the state when asked, so the hearing edges go in three layers of non-touching pairs, with a re-measurement before each layer. The engine then ran single- and two-qubit tomography of every hearing edge (1,024 shots each) and sampled the final state 1,024 times. All of this ran on Atlas's Aer simulator, a noiseless classical simulation of the circuit.

What this does not claim

Frogs are classical oscillators, and nothing here simulates a quantum frog. The engine's output is a fixed snapshot: its measured numbers set the couplings, starting phases and who is calling. The chorus itself is a classical Kuramoto model running in your browser.

Every engine number comes from Atlas's Aer simulator, not quantum hardware. qdrive-api-v1 accepted only aer: it answered that ibm_fez is "not wired up yet", and it rejected fake_fez. Locks kept the asked-for sign and most of their strength only where no later layer touched the same qubits. The rest were largely overwritten. We report that as it came out and claim no quantum advantage.

The pixel frogs are drawings. Their throats puff on the model's real call times and the threads follow the real locks; everything else they do (blinking, hopping, ripples) is decoration.

No VST or AU plugin is included. The instrument is this browser page (Web Audio), plus WAV renders.
