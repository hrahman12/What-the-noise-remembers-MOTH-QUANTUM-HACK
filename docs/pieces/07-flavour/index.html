Challenge 07 · Make a VST or AU · Neutrino flavour synth

Send a neutrino through the Earth. Hear which flavour arrives.

A neutrino is born one flavour and arrives as a mix of three, set by distance over energy. Pick a real source, drag the neutrino along its real baseline, and watch it light a water-Cherenkov detector. The muon-neutrino curves went onto qubits and came back from a real IBM chip, 12 IBM noise models and a noiseless simulator; the synth plays what came back.

  1. Pick a source: a nuclear reactor, an accelerator beam, cosmic rays in the atmosphere, or the Sun.
  2. Drag the neutrino along its real baseline (or arrow keys). The flavour bands follow the curves.
  3. Detect: click the tank or play a key. One neutrino interacts and lights a ring; the synth plays the mix.
  4. Swap chips and slide Mix from exact to measured to hear each machine's noise.

Model

The oscillation physics is classical. qpixl-v1 only encodes the muon-neutrino curves into qubits and measures them back. Chips named fake_* are noise models of IBM chips, run on a simulator; only ibm_fez is real hardware. Reactors and the Sun make electron-flavoured neutrinos, which no chip measured, so for them the page uses classical curves and says so. Each detection is a classical dice roll weighted by the curve at your spot. The rings are drawn with Super-K's geometry and the textbook Cherenkov angle to show how a water tank tells the flavours apart; they are an illustration, not a detector simulation.

Why “flavour”? It is what physicists call the three neutrino types. An earlier version of this page served them as gelato, and the plugin's editor still wears those names: KITCHEN = chip, RECIPE = mix, MENU = model, JOURNEY = L/E.

How to playKeys · curves · chips

Play the particle that changes identity mid-flight.

A muon neutrino turns into electron and tau neutrinos as it travels. We wrote its flavour curves onto qubits and read them back on a real IBM chip, 12 IBM noise models and a noiseless simulator. This synth plays what came back.

  1. Play the keys (or your computer keys A–K), or hold a drone.
  2. Fly: drag across the curves to set L/E, the distance travelled per unit of energy. Each flavour's loudness is its probability there.
  3. Switch CHIP and MIX to hear what each machine's noise did to the curves.

On a computer keyboard: A S D F G H J K L ; play the ten tubes, Z and X shift the octave. On the plate, the arrow keys move your neutrino (Shift for big steps, Home and End for the ends of the baseline) and Enter detects one; on the curves in The data, the arrow keys move it too.

PluginJUCE 8 · VST3 · AU · Standalone

The plugin

FLAVOUR is a JUCE 8 instrument plugin (VST3 on macOS and Windows, AU on macOS, plus a standalone app). The measured curves ship inside it as BinaryData, so it needs no network and makes no quantum calls at play time. It plays the muon-neutrino curves; the reactor and Sun sources are page-only.

The binaries come from the included GitHub Actions workflow (CMake + JUCE 8.0.8, checked with pluginval and auval). No plugin binary was built here, because this machine has no desktop C++ toolchain. Here, the plugin sources only type-check against the JUCE 8.0.8 headers, and the engine code, compiled into a test program, matches the browser and Python versions sample for sample. Until that workflow runs, the browser synth on this page and the Python replica are the playable versions.

How it was madeEngine · sound

How it was made

What the quantum engine did

Four curves (2-flavour P(μ→e), and 3-flavour P(μ→e), P(μ→μ), P(μ→τ)) are computed classically on a log-spaced L/E axis, each divided by its own maximum, and joined into one array. Atlas qpixl-v1 writes every number as a rotation angle on a data qubit, addressed by the states of neighbouring address qubits (Interwoven QPIXL), then measures and decodes them. We multiply the decoded values back by each curve's maximum. Nothing else is done to them.

Look at the ibm_fez dots: the errors come in short blocks of neighbouring points that sit too high or too low together. Neighbouring errors correlate at 0.88 on ibm_fez but only 0.06 on the noiseless simulator, and the error jumps where each 4-value block starts are about twice the jumps inside a block. That fits each block sharing one data qubit, whose own miscalibration shifts all its values at once. It's the chip's layout showing through the curve, and you can hear it as steps in the waveform. On the plate the same blocks show as steps in the flavour bands as you drag.

Every job, with where it ran, its size, its rms error against the exact curves and its Atlas job id, is listed under Jobs and credits.

How the sound is made

Three wavetable oscillators per note: νe a fifth above the key you play, νμ on it, ντ an octave below. Each oscillator's loudness is that flavour's probability at the current L/E. The keys are an A minor pentatonic, so any two sound fine together.

Each oscillator's waveform is the curve itself: half a decade of the flavour's curve around the current L/E, mirrored into one cycle. Smooth physics gives soft, rounded waves. Measurement noise shows up as jagged edges, which you hear as brighter, buzzier harmonics. MIX crossfades the exact curves into the measured ones, so you can hear exactly what each machine added. With the Sun selected the curves are flat (fully averaged), so the three oscillators become pure sine tones.

The science

Why a neutrino is a qubit you can't hold

A neutrino is made in one flavour (here a muon neutrino) but travels as a mix of three mass states. Each mass state picks up phase at its own rate, so the mix drifts and the flavour you would detect changes with distance L over energy E. (The reactor and the Sun on the plate make electron-flavoured ones instead; see the next drawer.)

With two flavours this is exactly a qubit. The mixing angle θ is a fixed change of basis, travel is a phase rotation by Δm²L/2E between the two mass states, and detection measures in the flavour basis. That gives P(νμ→νe) = sin²2θ · sin²(1.267 Δm²[eV²] L[km] / E[GeV]), the same form as a Rabi oscillation. Three flavours make it a qutrit, set by the 3×3 PMNS matrix.

The curves use the NuFIT 6.0 global-fit best values (normal ordering, the “IC19 without SK atmospheric data” column of Table 1): sin²θ12 = 0.307, sin²θ23 = 0.561, sin²θ13 = 0.02195, δCP = 177°, Δm²21 = 7.49×10−5 eV², Δm²31 = 2.534×10−3 eV². Interference terms are averaged over a 5% spread in L/E, as a detector's energy resolution would. Vacuum only: matter effects are left out.

Reactor and Sun: why their curves are classical

Every chip measured the same four curves, all for a neutrino born as a muon neutrino. A reactor makes electron antineutrinos and the Sun makes electron neutrinos, and their survival curve cannot be worked out from the muon-born ones. So for these two sources the bands and the dice use curves computed classically by the same code (physics.py, NuFIT 6.0, vacuum), and the Chip and Mix controls do nothing.

One bridge does exist. CPT symmetry makes P(ν̄e→ν̄μ) equal to P(νμ→νe), which the chips did measure, so with the reactor selected the readout puts the classical number next to the chosen chip's measured one at the same L/E.

The Sun is 1 AU away, so at solar energies L/E is about 1010 km/GeV, far past the right-hand end of the curves. There the oscillations average out completely and the page uses that average, Σi|Uei|²|Uαi|², which gives 55% νe. The real Sun delivers fewer: for the 8B neutrinos that Super-K sees, SNO measured about a third arriving as νe, because the Sun's own dense matter converts them on the way out (the MSW effect). This page leaves matter effects out, so the Sun card shows the vacuum number and names the gap.

HonestyPhysics · engine · limits

What this does not claim

The oscillation physics is computed classically. qpixl-v1 only encodes the curves into qubits and measures them back. Chips named fake_* are noise models of IBM chips, run on a simulator. Only ibm_fez is real hardware.

No neutrino is simulated on a quantum computer here. The physics is closed-form classical maths, and the quantum step is a data round trip: encode the curves into qubits, measure them, decode. The differences you hear between chips are qubit and readout noise (real on ibm_fez, modelled on fake_*), not neutrino physics. No quantum advantage is claimed.

The two-flavour curve is the standard leading-order approximation, not a separate measurement. The “Σ P” readout should be 1 for the exact 3-flavour curves; on measured curves it drifts because each value was measured independently. The demo audio comes from the Python replica of the plugin engine, not from a compiled plugin.

The curves are vacuum oscillations: matter effects are ignored, so they are not a prediction for T2K, NOvA, DUNE or the Sun, and the baselines are distances only. The detector drawn is Super-Kamiokande, T2K's far detector; MINOS, NOvA and DUNE use other kinds of detector, and Daya Bay, JUNO and KamLAND use liquid scintillator.

What the plate shows, and what it doesn't

Everything on the plate that looks like a number comes from the curves on this page: the flavour bands and their ΣP, the percentages at your neutrino, which flavour each detection draws, the noise squares on each chip, and where each held note has travelled to. For the beam and the atmosphere those are measured job outputs; for the reactor and the Sun they are classical and labelled so.

The drawings are original ink engravings made from published descriptions of the real objects (see Credits); no photograph is traced or embedded. They are not to scale, and the beamline's slope is drawn steeper than the real 3.3°. The event display places light with Super-K's real inner-detector size (33.8 m by 36.2 m) and the 42° Cherenkov angle, but the hit pattern is a simple sketch: charged-current events only, no neutral currents, no detector response, a few random dark hits. The wall carries about one dot for every four real tubes; low-energy events light about six tubes per MeV, as Super-K's do. Beam neutrinos enter from the left, and other directions are chosen so the ring faces the cut-away; for the atmosphere the zenith angle follows your distance.
