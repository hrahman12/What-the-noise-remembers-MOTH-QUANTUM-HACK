Challenge 02 · Make it audible

Swap hydrogen for deuterium. Hear the molecule fall.

A contested theory says your nose reads molecular vibrations. We slowed three odorants' vibrations 120 billion times into hearing range and ran them through a 20-qubit quantum blur. You get to smell by ear.

Unproven and contested

Nobody has shown that noses detect molecular vibrations. Experiments disagree, and hearing a shifted chord tells you nothing about smelling one.

  1. Pick a molecule and press Play to hear its vibrational chord.
  2. Swap H for D while it sounds: the hydrogen lines slide down by about a fourth.
  3. Blur the chord through the quantum circuit, from a light smear to a global scramble.
  4. Test yourself blind: which sample is deuterated?

Plate I is drawn as an anatomy plate; the odorants, atoms, neutrons and the tunnelling electron are pixel sprites in the same ink. The mascot sniffs when you do.

The instrument

  1. Pick a molecule: acetophenone (8 H), cyclopentadecanone / Exaltone (28 H) or muscone (30 H).
  2. Play its chord: the lines enter from the lowest to the highest frequency, then sustain.
  3. Swap H ↔ D while it sounds (button or the S key): the hydrogen lines glide down, live.
  4. Blur it: strength 0.25 / 0.5 / 1 × reach local / halfway / global, each one a real Atlas job. Click or drag the spectrum roll to scrub; arrows scrub, Space plays and pauses.
  5. WAV file: the instrument's "Play the WAV render" button plays the deliverable wav/<setting>/<molecule>_<H|D>.wav for whatever molecule, isotope and blur is selected (a 48 kb/s mp3 of it); it toggles to "Stop", the playhead follows it, and changing the selection (even H ↔ D) while it plays jumps to the matching file at the same moment.

The science

evidence for and against the vibration theory

Evidence for

Turin 1996: a spectroscope in the nose

Luca Turin proposed that olfactory receptors respond to an odorant's vibrations, read out by inelastic electron tunnelling: an electron crosses the receptor only if it can hand a vibrational quantum of the right energy to the molecule. He reported that acetophenone and acetophenone-d8 smell different.

L. Turin, "A spectroscopic mechanism for primary olfactory reception", Chemical Senses 21(6):773-791 (1996). doi:10.1093/chemse/21.6.773

Brookes et al. 2007: the physics could work

A simple but general model of phonon-assisted tunnelling, with parameters appropriate for biomolecules, found the proposed mechanism consistent with the underlying physics and with observed features of smell. This is a viability argument, not evidence that receptors do it.

J. C. Brookes, F. Hartoutsiou, A. P. Horsfield, A. M. Stoneham, "Could humans recognize odor by phonon assisted tunneling?", Physical Review Letters 98:038101 (2007). doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.98.038101

Franco et al. 2011: fruit flies tell isotopes apart

Drosophila distinguished normal from deuterated odorants and could be trained to avoid either one. Flies trained against a deuterated compound also avoided an unrelated molecule with a vibrational mode in the energy range of the carbon-deuterium stretch (Gane et al. later describe it as a nitrile). Shape alone does not explain that.

M. I. Franco, L. Turin, A. Mershin, E. M. C. Skoulakis, "Molecular vibration-sensing component in Drosophila melanogaster olfaction", PNAS 108(9):3797-3802 (2011). doi:10.1073/pnas.1012293108

Gane et al. 2013: humans and deuterated musk

With GC-purified samples, volunteers picked catalytically deuterated cyclopentadecanone over sham-deuterated cyclopentadecanone in 119 of 132 trials, describing a "burnt" note. The authors suggest that many C-H groups (28 here, against acetophenone's 8) may be needed for the difference to be smelled.

S. Gane, D. Georganakis, K. Maniati, M. Vamvakias, N. Ragoussis, E. M. C. Skoulakis, L. Turin, "Molecular vibration-sensing component in human olfaction", PLOS ONE 8(1):e55780 (2013). doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0055780

Evidence against

Keller & Vosshall 2004: no support in people

Psychophysical tests in humans of predictions made by the vibration theory, including whether acetophenone and acetophenone-d8 smell different, found no evidence to support it.

A. Keller, L. B. Vosshall, "A psychophysical test of the vibration theory of olfaction", Nature Neuroscience 7(4):337-338 (2004). doi:10.1038/nn1215

Gane et al. 2013: acetophenone stayed the same

The same paper that reported the musk result confirmed Keller & Vosshall: neither naive nor trained subjects could tell GC-pure acetophenone from acetophenone-d8. It also notes that deuteration changes physical properties: the deuterated musk left the GC column about 1.2 minutes earlier, a sign of weaker dispersion forces.

Gane et al., PLOS ONE 8(1):e55780 (2013). doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0055780

Block et al. 2015: the musk receptor does not care

The human musk receptor OR5AN1, expressed in cells, responded alike to normal and deuterated cyclopentadecanone and muscone; other human and mouse receptors also ignored deuteration. The authors note that muscone-d30 lacks the 1,380-1,550 cm-1 bands claimed to matter for musk odour, and argue from theory that the proposed electron transfer could easily be suppressed by quantum effects of the molecule's other, non-odorant vibrations.

E. Block et al., "Implausibility of the vibrational theory of olfaction", PNAS 112(21):E2766-E2774 (2015). doi:10.1073/pnas.1503054112

The 2015 exchange: still unresolved

Turin and colleagues replied that the theory remains plausible; Block and colleagues answered that it is implausible. The two letters were published side by side. As far as we know, no decisive experiment has settled the question since.

L. Turin, S. Gane, D. Georganakis, K. Maniati, E. M. C. Skoulakis, "Plausibility of the vibrational theory of olfaction", PNAS 112(25):E3154 (2015). doi:10.1073/pnas.1508035112
E. Block, S. Jang, H. Matsunami, V. S. Batista, H. Zhuang, "Reply to Turin et al.: Vibrational theory of olfaction is implausible", PNAS 112(25):E3155 (2015). doi:10.1073/pnas.1508443112

How it was made

from a vibration to a note to a quantum blur
How a vibration becomes a note

Each line is an approximate textbook infrared group frequency for the normal molecule, in wavenumbers (cm-1). One rule maps every line to sound: 1 cm-1 = 0.25 Hz, a slow-down of about 1.2 × 1011. Because the rule is a single multiplication, musical intervals are the true vibrational ratios.

Swapping hydrogen (mass 1) for deuterium (mass 2) changes the reduced mass of a C-H oscillator. For a harmonic bond the frequency falls by √(μCD/μCH) = 1.362, which is 5.35 semitones, a little more than a perfect fourth. We apply that factor to every line that moves a hydrogen and leave heavy-atom lines (C=O, ring C=C, C-C) where they are. Real deuterated spectra differ by a few percent, and modes mix; this is an idealised estimate, not a measurement.

For the quantum engine the chord is written as MIDI, with each MIDI note number standing for a 28 cm-1 bin (7 Hz), so lines are rounded by up to 14 cm-1. Your browser plays every note as a pure sine tone. The WAV file button instead plays the deliverable rendered offline in Python with the same rule (wav/<setting>/<molecule>_<H|D>.wav, compressed here to a 48 kb/s mp3) for whatever molecule, isotope and blur you have selected, so every one of the 60 chord files can be heard and checked against the live synth. All of this, including the synthesis, is classical.

The line table is Table 1 in The data.

What the quantum engine did

Atlas blur-midi-v1 (Quantum Blur for MIDI) turns each MIDI track into a piano roll (pitch × time) and blurs it as a height map with a quantum circuit, then turns the result back into notes. All six chords (three molecules, normal and deuterated) went into one file, one track each, so every job blurs H and D versions with exactly the same setting.

Qubits: each track's roll is about 77-111 pitch rows (the chord's span plus the engine's 15% margin) by 6,720 time steps (1 tick per step). Quantum Blur stores a w × h grid in ⌈log2 w⌉ + ⌈log2 h⌉ qubits: 7 + 13 = 20 qubits, the engine's maximum per pass. The engine does not report this number; we computed it from that rule.

Low reach mixes only neighbouring pitches and times, so lines smear into the bins next to them, a bit like a low-resolution spectrometer. Higher reach lets any pitch mix with any other: five of the six settings at reach 0.5 or 1 scatter a chord into dozens to thousands of notes across the range, while strength 1 at reach 1 leaves only a few dozen notes, many far from the original lines. In every job the deuterated chords stay below about 2,250 cm-1 while the normal ones reach the C-H stretch region, so the blur never fully hides the swap. This is an audio effect and an analogy, nothing more. The circuits ran on Atlas's classical statevector simulator; blur-midi-v1 has no hardware mode.

How the plates and sprites work

Plate I is a schematic anatomical drawing (after standard anatomy atlases, not traced from any one source); its labels use the English and Latin anatomical names. The molecules are pixel sprites drawn from their real structures: acetophenone (8 H), cyclopentadecanone (15-carbon ring, 28 H) and muscone (3-methyl, 30 H), with hydrogen drawn light and deuterium dark. H and D are drawn the same size on purpose: deuterium only adds a neutron.

The mascot (a head in midline section, sniffing acetophenone) and the blind test's nose are drawn from the same sprite sheet; the hub icon is exported from it. The loupe on Plate I shows the odorant docked at the second landing spot at molecular scale.

While a chord plays, every atom's wobble is driven by the notes sounding at the playhead, from the same note lists you hear (input score or downloaded blur-midi-v1 output): C-H or C-D stretch notes shake the hydrogens along their bonds, bend notes shake them sideways, C=O notes move the oxygen, ring notes breathe the ring. The wobble is slowed about 1013 times further than the sound, so a C-D bond visibly wobbles 1.36 times slower than a C-H bond. Rungs in Fig. d light up when their note sounds. The caption under Plate I names the loudest note sounding, from the same list. The sniff (and its sound, filtered noise), the docking, the neutrons that fly in when you swap H for D, the spikes in Fig. b, the flash of the bulb, the tunnelling electron and the kick it gives the odorant in Fig. d are illustrations, not measurements. Each hydrogen does gain exactly one neutron, and the molar masses shown are computed from the formulas.

What this does not claim

honesty notes

The vibration theory of olfaction is unproven and contested. This page does not test it, support it or refute it. The quantum circuit did not simulate electron tunnelling, an odorant receptor or a nose. Its blur is a sound effect applied to a chord. Being able to hear deuterium in a chord that we deliberately moved into hearing range says nothing about whether noses detect it. Band positions are approximate textbook values and the deuterated lines are harmonic estimates. The blind test scores you against chance; it is not a psychophysics experiment.

Fig. d illustrates the hypothesis; the receptor gap is a slider, not a measured property of any receptor. The sniff, the docking, the neutrons, the spikes, the flash of the bulb and the tunnelling electron are illustrations, not measurements.
