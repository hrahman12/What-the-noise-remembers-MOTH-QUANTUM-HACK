Challenge 02 · Make it audible

Scrub through the longest run a five-state machine can make.

This Turing machine halts after 47,176,870 steps, and in 2024 it was proved that no five-state machine that halts runs longer. Its tape plays as a score, and a quantum blur, run at the engine's 20-qubit maximum, turns that score into echoes. Its head is a beaver in a hard hat, its tape is a river it dams one log at a time, and every hop plays a note.

The scienceBB(5) = 47,176,870
Why this machine?

A busy beaver is the five-state, two-symbol Turing machine that runs longest from a blank tape and still halts. This one, found by Heiner Marxen and Jürgen Buntrock in 1989 (Bulletin of the EATCS 40, 1990), halts after 47,176,870 steps with 4,098 ones on its tape.

For 34 years nobody could rule out a longer-running halter. In 2024 the bbchallenge collaboration settled it with a proof checked in Coq: BB(5) = 47,176,870 (“Determination of the fifth Busy Beaver value”, arXiv:2509.12337, 2025). That is the first new busy beaver value in over 40 years.

The machine is 1RB1LC_1RC1RB_1RD0LE_1LA1LD_1RZ0LA in the bbchallenge format, which is a left-right mirror of Marxen and Buntrock's table, so here the tape grows to the left. Its run is a Collatz-like loop. A block of x ones is read as x = 3k or 3k + 1 and rebuilt as a block of 5k + 6 or 5k + 9 ones, until x = 12,284 = 3k + 2 and it halts. That gives 0 → 6 → 16 → 34 → … → 7,366 → 12,284 → halt: 15 phrases. Each phrase is a widening zigzag. The head turns the block into a 1 0 0 1 0 0 pattern, then zigzags outward, filling one 1 0 0 unit on each round trip.

How it was madescore mapping · quantum blur
How 47 million steps become 4,574 sixteenth notes

Phrases. The run splits exactly at the 15 moments when the tape is a single block of ones with the head in state A at its left end. We checked every split in the simulation. Phrase k gets round(15 × 1.41k) notes: the first phrase plays all 15 of its steps, and the 14th packs 30.2 million steps into 1,306 notes. The coda gets 96 notes, then a 24-note final chord.

Inside a phrase. If it has 2 or more notes per head sweep, each sweep gets its turnaround and then cells evenly spaced along it. Otherwise the sweeps are sampled evenly in right/left pairs, and each sampled sweep gives its turnaround and its midpoint. That makes the low, centre, high, centre figure you hear, and stops 4,916 sweeps aliasing into noise. Every note is a real head position at a real step: the readout shows which step.

Pitch. Left is low. The cells the head visits in a phrase are spread over D minor pentatonic, and the range widens as the tape grows: 6 notes in phrase 1, 21 (four octaves, A2 to A6) by the end. The bass plays which branch of the rule is running (x = 3k: D, x = 3k + 1: B♭). The final chord sounds every note of the range, because the halted tape is a 1 0 0 pattern spread evenly across all of it.

What the quantum engine did

Atlas blur-midi-v1 (Quantum Blur for MIDI) read our MIDI file, built a piano roll (pitch × time) for each track, and encoded it as amplitudes on a Gray-coded qubit register. It applied a rotation to each qubit, read the blurred roll back and turned it into notes. Strength sets the rotation; reach 0 mixes only neighbouring pitches and times, and 1 lets any cell mix with any other.

Every job used qubits = 20, the engine's maximum budget per pass. The engine does not report how many qubits it actually used, so we computed it with QuantumBlur's grid rule, ⌈log2 time steps⌉ + ⌈log2 pitch rows⌉. The melody spans MIDI 45–93. The outputs reach down to 38, exactly the engine's 15% margin, so its roll has 56 pitch rows, or 63 if the margin is padded above too. Either way that is 6 qubits. On the 1/16-note grid (4,574 steps, 13 qubits) that comes to 19 qubits per pass. On the 1/32-note grid (resolution 60 ticks, 9,148 steps, 14 qubits) it is 20, the whole budget. That is why we ran the gentle blur on both grids. The bass roll (MIDI 34–45 in the raw score, down to 32 in the outputs) needs fewer. All jobs ran on Atlas's classical statevector simulator, not on a quantum computer.

The six jobs, with their settings, qubit counts, note counts and job IDs, are listed under “Jobs and credits”.

What this does not claimhonesty

The machine, the score and the sound are classical. Python ran the machine to make the score, and your browser re-runs all 47,176,870 steps to draw the tape. The quantum blur ran on a simulator, it computes nothing about the machine, and it shows no quantum advantage. It is a sound effect with a quantum circuit inside. The time compression and pitch mapping are our artistic choices, explained above. When more than 10 notes start on the same grid step, which happens almost everywhere in the strength-0.5, reach-1 blur (about 23 per sixteenth), the page plays only the 10 loudest so your browser keeps up (the final chord gets up to 32). It still draws them all, and the three WAV renders in “Hear the full renders” sound every note.

The beaver scene is a drawing of the real run, not a simulation of anything else. Where the beaver stands, what its hat says, which cells hold logs, how big the far dam is and every ring in the echo pool come from the machine and the score on this page. The trees, clouds, waves, wood chips and sparkles are decoration, and the dive between far-apart cells stands for the thousands of steps a time-lapse hop skips.
