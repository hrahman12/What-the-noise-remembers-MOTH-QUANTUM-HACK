An Ising toy on a connectome graph, not a brain simulation. Lamps are spins, not neurons firing. How to play

Pull 20 spins into line with a fly’s own synapses.

Real wiring from the hemibrain connectome sets the couplings of an Ising toy. A classical Gibbs sampler and a 20-qubit Atlas circuit each show which spins fall into step, and where they disagree.

  1. Pick a circuit: the compass, memory or smell neurons.
  2. Explore the wiring: tap a neuron to see its partners and synapse counts.
  3. Switch sampler and temperature, then step through the spin patterns each one returns.
  4. Compare correlations: tap any cell to light up that pair on the wiring.

These four steps work on the metro above and on the results bench in The data below: both show the same circuit, sampler, sample and neuron, so a change in one moves the other.

03

How it was made

What the quantum engine did

Atlas graph-v1 (built on QuantumGraph) prepares a 20-qubit state from targets and samples it. Every qubit starts at |+⟩ (spin up or down with equal odds). Then each bond, strongest first, gets a two-qubit target ⟨ZZ⟩ = +1 applied as a partial rotation. The fraction is chosen so that, acting alone on two fresh qubits, it would give exactly the thermal value tanh(βJ). Inside a loopy graph the rotations act on qubits that are already entangled, so the prepared state is not the Boltzmann distribution. That gap is what you see when you switch samplers.

–

We rebuilt each circuit classically as an exact 220-amplitude statevector using the same rule (the "Exact state" sampler, labelled classical). The emulator's sampled patterns match it. graph-v1 also reports a "tomography" of the state; its pairwise ⟨ZZ⟩ values are far weaker than the exact state and the samples, so we treat that report as an approximation and do not use it for the correlations.

In the metro scene the emulator's samples ride the Atlas maglev, and the "Exact state" sampler is the Exact blueprint train. The ibm_fez hardware samples ride the solid-ink IBM train.

How to read the plates, the map and the fly

The scene is three linked plates, like a page of an anatomy atlas. FIG. 1A is a male Drosophila melanogaster seen from above, about 2.2 mm long with a 1.9 mm wing (the scale bar is 1 mm). FIG. 1B is its brain seen from the front, about 630 × 290 µm like the JRC2018 template brain, with the fly’s right on your left. The hemibrain imaged most of the right half of the central brain and part of the left; the pale bands lie outside it. The box shows the region FIG. 1C enlarges, and the dots in it are the 20 stations, lit or dark like the lamps.

In FIG. 1C each station is one real neuron, named by its hemibrain cell type plus the side or column from its instance name (the station card gives the full instance and body ID). Behind the map are the neuropils (brain regions) of that part of the brain: the protocerebral bridge, fan-shaped body, ellipsoid body and noduli of the central complex; the calyx, peduncle and the α, α′, β, β′ and γ lobes of the mushroom body; the antennal lobe with its glomeruli and the lateral horn. Each station sits in or next to its home region: the region holding the most of its synapses, counted over all its partners in the hemibrain v1.2 ROI table (roi_profile.py). Where the cell-type name pins a finer place, the station is pulled towards it: a bridge glomerulus (PEG L3 sits at glomerulus L3 and Δ7 L2R7 between L2 and R7; R9 to R1 run on your left, L1 to L9 on your right), a mushroom-body compartment (MBON09(γ3β′1) sits at γ3), or an antennal-lobe glomerulus (DP1m adPN, VM7d adPN). Tap a station: its home region gets a dashed outline, and the card lists its next regions and where each bond’s synapses lie.

The neuropil outlines are our own schematic of a frontal view (anatomy.py), drawn from published descriptions with approximate proportions and depth flattened; they are not a mesh from the data. Stations snap to an octilinear grid (metro_layout.py, seeded simulated annealing) so the lines run at 0, 45 or 90 degrees like a transit map, and no line runs through a station it does not serve. That is why some sit just outside their home region: in each network at least 10 of the 20 are inside it or on its edge, and the farthest is 46 µm out (the station card gives each distance). Grid steps are true micrometres on the plate (see the scale bar), but a line’s length means nothing.

Each metro line is one of the 30 bonds in the model, and its weight follows the synapse count between the two cells (square-root scale, thinnest to thickest within each network). Single lines are the maximum spanning tree; double lines are the 11 extra strongest pairs that close loops. When you pick a station, faint dotted lines show its real connections that the model leaves out. A lit lamp is spin +1 and a dark lamp is spin −1 in the sample on show. When you step, the lamps switch in a wave that spreads from the train along the lines: that timing is decoration, and the pattern it settles on is the real sample.

The fly follows the real anatomy: compound eyes of about 700 hexagonal facets each in a male, three ocelli in a triangle on the vertex, antennae with plumose aristae, the orbital, ocellar, postvertical and vertical bristles of the head, all 26 large bristles of the dorsal thorax (humerals, presutural, notopleurals, supra-alars, postalars, two pairs of dorsocentrals, and the basal and crossing apical scutellars), rows of acrostichal hairs, wings with the costa and its breaks, veins L1 to L6 and the anterior and posterior crossveins, halteres (the hind wings, reduced to balancers), six legs with five tarsal segments and claws, the sex comb on each male foreleg, and the banded abdomen with the male’s dark A5 and A6. Its mood is decoration: when at least 29 of 30 lines are in step it buzzes its wings as each new sample arrives, it grooms when 22 or fewer are, and otherwise it stands. Flies do not do this. The semaphore beside the controls raises its arm as a sample departs and lights its lamp while you ride. The optional chime plays two notes per sample; the interval widens as more lines run in step.

The web app

This page replays cached runs. The piece also ships a small Node server (app/server.js, no dependencies) that serves this page and proxies graph-v1 calls to Atlas from the server side. The API key stays in the project's .env on the server and is never sent to the browser. It is not deployed. New jobs are off unless the operator sets ALLOW_NEW_JOBS=1, and the piece's 40-credit cap still applies.

04

The science

Open a drawer
Why a fly brain?

The hemibrain is an electron-microscopy reconstruction of a large part of one fruit fly's central brain, published by Janelia's FlyEM team and Google (Scheffer et al., eLife 2020). The openly downloadable v1.2 adjacency export lists 21,739 traced neurons and 3,550,403 directed neuron-to-neuron connections, each with a synapse count.

We grew three densely wired 20-neuron sets from it: the head-direction compass of the central complex, the hubs of the mushroom body's memory circuit, and the lateral-horn smell pathway. Twenty is the most qubits Atlas graph-v1 accepts.

How synapses become couplings

For each pair, w = synapses one way + synapses the other way. The modelled graph is the maximum spanning tree (19 bonds) plus the next 11 strongest pairs: 30 bonds per circuit. Each bond gets J = w / (median w of the 30 bonds), so a typical bond has J = 1. All couplings are ferromagnetic: the export has no sign (excitatory or inhibitory), so the toy only asks which neurons are wired strongly enough to pull their spins into step.

The Gibbs sampler targets p(s) ∝ exp(β Σ Jij si sj). The faint thin lines on the graph are real connections that were left out of the model.

05

What this does not claim

An Ising toy on a connectome graph, not a brain simulation. Spins are not neurons firing.

This is an Ising toy on a connectome graph, not a simulation of a brain. Spins are not neurons firing, and β is not a biological temperature. Couplings ignore synapse sign, neurotransmitter, timing and every neuron outside the 20. The quantum circuit is a state-preparation recipe, not a quantum Gibbs sampler, and nothing here shows a quantum advantage. There is no hardware data. The emulator and "Exact state" are classical simulations of a quantum circuit. The brief asked for hemibrain v1.2.1, which is only reachable through neuPrint with a sign-in token, so this uses the openly downloadable v1.2 export.

In the metro scene the "Exact state" sampler is the "Exact" blueprint train. The trains, the semaphore, the light wave, the fly's mood and the chime are decoration, not measurements. The fly and the neuropil outlines are our own drawings from published descriptions, with approximate proportions; they are not renderings of the hemibrain data. What is data on the plates: which region each station sits in or next to (the hemibrain ROI table), the lines and their weights, and the lamps.
