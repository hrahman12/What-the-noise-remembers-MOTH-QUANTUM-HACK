## Papers

- B. P. Abbott et al. (LIGO Scientific and Virgo Collaborations), "Observation of Gravitational Waves
  from a Binary Black Hole Merger", *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 116, 061102 (2016).
  The game's alert card and drawers quote its published estimates, checked against the abstract and text
  (arXiv:1602.03837): 14 Sep 2015 at 09:50:45 UTC; 36 and 29 solar masses merging into 62; 3.0 solar masses
  radiated; peak luminosity 3.6 × 10⁵⁶ erg/s (200 M☉c²/s); luminosity distance 410 Mpc (z = 0.09); 35 to 250 Hz;
  peak strain 1.0 × 10⁻²¹; SNR 24; false-alarm rate below 1 per 203 000 years (> 5.1σ); L1 first, H1 6.9 ms
  later; reported by low-latency searches within three minutes. The detector numbers on the plates come from
  the same paper: 20 W into the interferometer, 700 W on the beam splitter, 100 kW in each arm, 40 kg
  fused-silica test masses on quadruple pendulums with fused-silica fibres, 1.2 m tubes below 1 µPa. They are
  quoted, not measured here. The later GWTC-1 values on the GWOSC event page (35.6 + 30.6 → 63.1 M☉,
  3.1 M☉c², 440 Mpc) are also quoted in a drawer.
- D. Ganapathy et al. (LIGO O4 Detector Collaboration), "Broadband Quantum Enhancement of the LIGO
  Detectors with Frequency-Dependent Squeezing", *Phys. Rev. X* 13, 041021 (2023),
  doi:10.1103/PhysRevX.13.041021. We checked the figures quoted (4.0 dB at Hanford, 5.8 dB at
  Livingston, 300 m filter cavity, 15–18 % range, up to 65 % detection rate) against the abstract on the
  NSF PAR record.
- M. Tse et al., "Quantum-Enhanced Advanced LIGO Detectors in the Era of Gravitational-Wave Astronomy",
  *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 123, 231107 (2019): squeezing in O3, up to 3 dB above 50 Hz (checked against the abstract).
- D. Griffin and J. Lim, "Signal estimation from modified short-time Fourier transform",
  *IEEE Trans. Acoustics, Speech, and Signal Processing* 32(2), 236–243 (1984).
- J. R. Wootton, "Procedural generation using quantum computation", *Proc. 15th International Conference
  on the Foundations of Digital Games (FDG '20)* (2020), the Quantum Blur method behind Atlas's blur engines.

## Reference sources consulted for the game and the drawings

The plates, the black holes and the mascot are original drawings in the shared palette. No photograph was
embedded, traced or copied; these sources were read for facts, dimensions and part names only.

- GWOSC event page for GW150914 (GPS 1126259462.4, GWTC-1 parameters):
  https://gwosc.org/eventapi/html/GWTC-1-confident/GW150914/v3/
- GWOSC detector constants (LAL), used for Livingston's arm bearings (X 252.3°, Y 162.3°, computed from the
  arm unit vectors): https://gwosc.org/s/param/position.txt
- B. P. Abbott et al., the discovery paper above (arXiv:1602.03837), for the event facts and the detector
  configuration on the plates.
- LIGO Laboratory, "Facts": https://www.ligo.caltech.edu/page/facts (4 km arms; the 3 mm steel beam tubes with
  stiffening rings about every metre; about a trillionth of an atmosphere; the slab levelled for the Earth's
  curvature of nearly a metre over 4 km).
- LIGO Laboratory, "What is LIGO?" and "Facilities": https://www.ligo.caltech.edu/LA/page/what-is-ligo and
  https://www.ligo.caltech.edu/page/facilities (the concrete enclosure over the tubes; the corner station).
- D. Shoemaker, "LIGO project update", *Matters of Gravity* 13 (1999), arXiv:gr-qc/9902011 (from space, the
  Livingston arms show as clearings in the forest and the main building is X-shaped; the beam-tube cover is
  arch-shaped).
- T. Commissariat, "The great detector: a visit to LIGO Livingston", *Physics World* (16 Oct 2017),
  https://physicsworld.com/a/the-great-detector-a-visit-to-ligo-livingston/ (dense pine forest; stray bullets
  hitting the arms in the early days; the control room is staffed day and night).
- A. Cho, "To catch a wave", *Science* 347, 1084 (2015), as summarised in search results (a loblolly-pine
  working forest around the arms, and logging vibrations the instrument must reject).
- LIGO Scientific and Virgo Collaborations, "Localization and broadband follow-up of the gravitational-wave
  transient GW150914", *Astrophys. J. Lett.* 826, L13 (2016), arXiv:1602.08492 (GW150914 came during engineering
  run ER8, four days before the first observing run, O1, formally began on 18 September 2015).
- Ganapathy et al. 2023 and Tse et al. 2019 above, for the squeezer (an OPO with a PPKTP crystal pumped at
  532 nm, injected through the output Faraday isolator; squeezing from O3, a 300 m filter cavity from O4).
