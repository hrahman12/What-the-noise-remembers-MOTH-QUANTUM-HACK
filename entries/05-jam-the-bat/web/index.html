    How to playThe rule the bat plays by

    The game rule

    While you hold, gaps between clicks are 70 ms + slot × 12 ms. The bat turns each gap into one of 12 symbols (8 slots, plus 4 lengths of pause) and predicts the next one with an order-3 n-gram: counts of what followed your last three symbols, backing off to shorter contexts until one has at least 4 examples, with add-half smoothing. The meter shows the probability it gives its best guess.

    A click the bat predicted is tuned out. A click it got wrong jams every echo for the next 160 ms. Clean echoes give it your position and tighten its fix. Jammed echoes leave it flying at a stale guess (the dashed ring). In the terminal buzz it strikes at that guess. It catches you if you're within 24 px, and it breaks off if its fix falls below 20%. Clicks cost breath, and breath only comes back after 250 ms of silence.

    On the stage, a click the bat predicted makes it swoop, and a click it got wrong shows a zap and breaks up its sonar arcs. The ghost moth inside the dashed ring is its stale guess.

    With the seed leaked, the bat replays your PRNG: it has the seed and counts your clicks, so it knows every next slot and only guesses whether you'll keep holding. The flight, the bat and the tournament are all classical code. So are the sprites.

    How it was madecomet-qrng-v1, then classical code

    What the quantum engine did

    Atlas comet-qrng-v1 puts every register qubit in |+⟩ and measures it 10,000 times. On IBM hardware it used 148 register qubits + 8 Bell-witness qubits = 156, every qubit on the chip (the count comes from the job's own provenance record). It reads only counts, so the order of the shots is lost. It estimates a conservative per-bit min-entropy with NIST SP 800-90B estimators, subtracts log2(10,000!) ≈ 118,458 bits for the lost shot order, and runs a Toeplitz extractor whose output is 2-64-close to uniform given that budget.

    Each click's jitter is the next 3 bits of those bytes, a slot from 0 to 7, which adds 0 to 84 ms to the gap. Cutting bytes into 3-bit slots is a classical step. Nothing else is done to the bits. The three lights on the pixel chip and the emulator screen show exactly those 3 bits for the click just fired.

    The emulator bank is the same pipeline on the Qiskit Aer simulator, which the engine caps at 20 qubits. A 12 + 8 run (12 register qubits plus the 8 witness qubits) returned 0 bytes: 12 qubits × 10,000 shots carry less entropy than the shot-order penalty takes away. A 20 + 0 run fills the emulator bank instead, and the 12 + 8 run supplies the emulator's ideal S value.

    The scienceSonar jamming, in the lab and the field

    Why a tiger moth?

    Grote's tiger moth, Bertholdia trigona, answers an attacking big brown bat with bursts of ultrasonic clicks. Corcoran, Barber & Conner (Science 325:325-327, 2009) filmed and recorded these duels and showed the clicks jam the bat's sonar. In the field, bats caught more than ten times as many moths with their sound organs silenced as clicking moths (Corcoran & Conner, J. Exp. Biol. 215:4278-4287, 2012).

    A hunting bat moves through three phases: a slow search call, an approach where calls speed up, and a terminal buzz just before the strike (Griffin, Webster & Michael, Animal Behaviour 8:141-154, 1960). The bat in this game follows the same three phases.

    The rest is a game. These papers do not show that bats learn a moth's click rhythm. That part is our cartoon, so that the quality of your randomness can matter. Real calls and clicks are ultrasonic. Here they're pitched down so you can hear them, and time is stretched so a human can play. The striped pixel moth is a cartoon too.

    HonestyRead this before the numbers

    What this does not claim

    The hardware bank does not beat the emulator or a secret-seed PRNG in this game, and it shouldn't. An n-gram can't tell good sources apart, and the tournament shows them level within error. What loses is predictability: a metronome, or a seed the bat has read.

    The hardware bytes are accounted, not certified. Their entropy budget assumes no dependence between qubits beyond pairwise correlations. The engine names that assumption and tests it only by collision counts. Its assumption-free budget is 0 bits. The bytes are also public: they ship inside this page. A bat that read the page source could replay them exactly like the leaked seed. The emulator bank is classical pseudo-randomness, an uncertified baseline.
