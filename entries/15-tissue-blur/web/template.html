    Why a mouse brain's genes?

    Spatial transcriptomics measures which genes are switched on in each cell without losing where that cell sits. This map is one sagittal slice of an adult mouse brain from the whole-brain MERFISH atlas of Zhang et al. (Nature, 2023): animal 4, section 3, 95,104 cells and 1,120 genes in this release, read out by imaging. Front of the brain is on the left, the cerebellum on the right.

    • Three territories: Satb2 (mostly cortex), Gpr88 (striatum), Gabra6 (granule layer of the cerebellum).
    • Folds and tracts: Fezf2 (deeper cortical layers, among other places), C1ql2 (the dentate gyrus of the hippocampus), Mog (myelinating oligodendrocytes, so the fibre tracts).

    The map itself is classical. Each cell's normalised expression value is placed at its position and smoothed with a Gaussian (1.6 px, about 17 µm). Each gene is then scaled so its 99.5th percentile over the tissue is full brightness, and stored as one 8-bit colour channel of a 1024 × 1024 PNG: three genes per image. The values are relative brightness, not molecule counts.

    On the page each gene is drawn as a pigment on paper (more expression, more ink), and the faint tint behind them is the tissue outline, found from cell density. Both are classical display choices made in your browser; the probe and the table read the raw pixel values.

    How it was madeAtlas blur-v1 · 20 qubits · simulator
    What the quantum engine did

    Atlas blur-v1 takes the image and a mask. For each connected mask region, and each colour channel on its own, it encodes the pixel brightnesses into the amplitudes of a quantum state, on a register where neighbouring pixels differ by one bit (QuantumBlur's Gray-code layout). It then applies one rotation gate per qubit and reads the probabilities back out as pixels. Outside the mask, every output pixel equals the input pixel (checked for all 8 jobs).

    Qubits. A lens is a disk with a 541 × 541 px bounding box: ⌈log₂ 541⌉ + ⌈log₂ 541⌉ = 10 + 10 = 20 qubits, the engine's maximum. The whole-section mask's box is 1012 × 575 px, which is also 10 + 10 = 20. These counts come from the engine's documented rule. The engine does not report a qubit count.

    Three genes per job. The engine documents that colour channels are processed one at a time, so each job blurs three gene maps. What we see agrees with that: in the front lens, the Gabra6 channel stays at or below 19/255 even though Satb2 and Gpr88 fill the lens.

    What the settings did here. All jobs use strength 1.0 with rx gates. At reach 0 (local), detail inside the lens regroups into square blocks but the layout survives: agreement r - wherever the gene is present. At reach 0.5, the lens becomes a plaid of blocks: r -, and - of the blurred brightness lands where the gene was not detected.

    blur-v1 runs on Atlas's classical statevector simulator. It has no QPU mode, so none of this ran on quantum hardware.

    Meet the cast

    The pixel-art cast is drawn in the page's ink palette. Some of it is driven by the job data and some of it is decoration. Here is which is which.

      Ghost spots are found classically in build_web.py: the brightest blurred pixels (local maxima at least 41 px apart) among pixels that were dark (< 4/255) in the measured map, inside the mask. Gene tags sit on each gene's densest measured spot (Gaussian-smoothed, 16 px). The mouse's mood follows the mean agreement r of the genes in view: happy at 0.8 or more, unsure from 0.5, dizzy below 0.5. Catching a ghost changes nothing in the data.

      What this does not claimHonesty notes

      The blur is a visual effect, not an analysis method. It does not denoise, impute, cluster or smooth expression for statistics. It moves brightness between pixel positions according to the bit patterns of their addresses. The ghost-signal column shows it placing expression where none was measured. Don't read any biology into a blurred pixel. The ghosts in the scene show the same thing, pixel by pixel.

      How the two numbers are computed (classical, from the downloaded images, inside the mask). Agreement r is the Pearson correlation between measured and blurred pixel values for that gene. Ghost signal is the share of that gene's brightness sitting on pixels that were dark (below 4/255) in the measured map, shown as measured → blurred.

      No quantum advantage or speed-up is claimed: the simulator computes these images on classical hardware. Display colours are added in your browser. The lens snaps to the three computed positions, and nothing is computed live. The 8-credit cap allowed 8 jobs, so reach 0.5 exists only for Three territories in the centre lens, and the whole-section mask only for Three territories. While you drag, the faint rim only previews where it will snap. The mouse, its moods and the cell drawings are illustrations.
